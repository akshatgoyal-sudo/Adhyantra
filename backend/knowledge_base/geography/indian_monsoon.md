# Indian Monsoon

## Simple Explanation
The Indian Monsoon is a seasonal reversal of winds associated with large-scale atmospheric circulation and differential heating of land and sea. It is central to India's climate, agriculture, water resources, and economy.

## Key Points
- The southwest monsoon brings most of India's annual rainfall between June and September.
- Differential heating, the ITCZ, pressure changes, jet streams, and the Tibetan Plateau all influence monsoon behaviour.
- Monsoon rainfall is uneven in both space and time, producing flood-prone and drought-prone regions.
- The retreating or northeast monsoon is especially important for parts of southeast India.
- Monsoon variability affects agriculture, food prices, hydropower, and rural livelihoods.

## Exam Relevance
UPSC often asks about the mechanism, branches, onset, retreat, irregularity, and economic significance of the monsoon. It also connects monsoon questions with climate, agriculture, and disaster management.
