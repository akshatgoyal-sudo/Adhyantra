# Revolt of 1857

## Simple Explanation
The Revolt of 1857 was a major anti-colonial uprising against the East India Company. It began with sepoy discontent but soon took the shape of a wider political and social challenge in north and central India.

## Key Points
- Immediate military grievances acted as the trigger, but deeper political and economic causes already existed.
- The revolt saw participation from sepoys, dispossessed rulers, zamindars, peasants, and townspeople in different regions.
- Delhi became a symbolic centre when Bahadur Shah II was declared the leader of the revolt.
- The revolt was strong in north and central India but weak or absent in many southern and eastern areas.
- After suppressing the revolt, the British Crown took direct control from the East India Company in 1858.

## Exam Relevance
UPSC commonly asks about the causes, character, leadership, limits, and consequences of the Revolt of 1857. Questions often test whether it was merely a sepoy mutiny or a broader political uprising.

## Practice Questions
- Why is the Revolt of 1857 described as both a military and political uprising?
- What were the major structural causes behind the Revolt of 1857?
- Why did the revolt remain regionally limited?
- How did the revolt change British administration in India?
- In what ways does UPSC distinguish between the revolt's immediate trigger and deeper causes?
