# Citizenship

## Simple Explanation
Citizenship defines the formal legal membership of a person in the Indian state. It determines who enjoys full political rights and who is recognized as a member of the constitutional community.

## Key Points
- The Constitution addresses citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution in Articles 5 to 11.
- Parliament can regulate citizenship further through ordinary law.
- India follows single citizenship rather than dual citizenship at the Union and state levels.
- Citizenship issues often involve birth, descent, registration, naturalization, and loss of citizenship.
- UPSC regularly tests constitutional articles together with the Citizenship Act and current developments.

## Exam Relevance
Citizenship is a high-yield area because prelims questions often mix constitutional provisions with statutory rules. It is also useful in mains when discussing migration, inclusion, and constitutional identity.

## Practice Questions
- Which articles of the Constitution deal with citizenship at commencement?
- Why does India follow single citizenship?
- How is citizenship under the Constitution different from citizenship under statute?
- What are the common modes of acquiring citizenship?
- Why does UPSC connect citizenship with constitutional identity?
