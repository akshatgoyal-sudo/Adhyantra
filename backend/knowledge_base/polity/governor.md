# Governor

## Simple Explanation
The Governor is the constitutional head of a state. Although real executive authority is exercised by the Chief Minister and the Council of Ministers, the Governor plays an important role in state administration, legislation, and certain discretionary situations.

## Key Points
- The Governor is appointed by the President.
- The office functions as the nominal head of the state executive.
- Most powers are exercised on the aid and advice of the state Council of Ministers.
- The Governor has legislative, executive, and discretionary functions in limited cases.
- UPSC often asks about the Governor's discretionary powers and the office's role in Centre-state relations.

## Exam Relevance
This topic is important because it connects federalism, parliamentary government at the state level, and constitutional conventions. Questions often focus on appointment, tenure, discretion, ordinances, and reports under Article 356.

## Practice Questions
- Why is the Governor described as the constitutional head of the state?
- What are the major discretionary powers of the Governor?
- How is the Governor linked to Article 356?
- Why does the Governor often appear in questions on Centre-state relations?
- How is the Governor different from the President?
