# Rivers of India

## Simple Explanation
Rivers of India are usually studied through two broad systems: the Himalayan rivers and the Peninsular rivers. They are important for water supply, irrigation, transport, ecology, and regional development.

## Key Points
- Himalayan rivers are generally perennial and carry large volumes of water and sediment.
- Peninsular rivers are mostly rain-fed, have more fixed courses, and flow over older geological surfaces.
- Drainage patterns reflect relief, structure, slope, and geological history.
- River systems support agriculture, settlements, industry, and hydroelectric development.
- UPSC often links rivers with disputes, floods, interlinking debates, and delta formation.

## Exam Relevance
UPSC tests classification of river systems, drainage features, river valleys, deltas, and the practical significance of rivers in Indian geography. It often asks comparative questions between Himalayan and Peninsular rivers.

## Practice Questions
- How do Himalayan rivers differ from Peninsular rivers?
- Why are drainage patterns important in physical geography?
- What is the significance of rivers beyond irrigation?
- Why are river systems important in inter-state and environmental debates?
- How does UPSC frame comparative questions on Indian rivers?
