# Directive Principles

## Simple Explanation
Directive Principles of State Policy are constitutional guidelines that tell the government what kind of welfare-oriented society it should try to build. They are not enforceable in court, but they are meant to shape policy and lawmaking.

## Key Points
- They are contained in Part IV of the Constitution.
- They are non-justiciable, but still fundamental in the governance of the country.
- Their goal is to create social and economic justice and reduce inequality.
- They often influence welfare laws, legal aid measures, and public policy design.
- UPSC frequently tests the balance between Directive Principles and Fundamental Rights.

## Exam Relevance
This topic helps in understanding the Constitution's social justice vision. Exam questions usually focus on enforceability, constitutional part numbers, and how Directive Principles interact with enforceable rights.

## Practice Questions
- Why are Directive Principles called non-justiciable?
- How do Directive Principles support the idea of a welfare state?
- What is the difference between Directive Principles and Fundamental Rights?
- Which examples of public policy reflect Directive Principles?
- Why does UPSC test harmony between Parts III and IV?
