# President

## Simple Explanation
The President is the formal head of the Union executive. Although real executive power is exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers, the office remains constitutionally significant in appointments, legislation, and emergencies.

## Key Points
- The President is elected indirectly by an electoral college.
- The office is a part of Parliament and also the Union executive structure.
- Most presidential functions are exercised on ministerial advice.
- The President has legislative, executive, judicial, military, and emergency-related powers.
- UPSC often tests the difference between nominal authority and actual executive power.

## Exam Relevance
Questions usually focus on election, impeachment, pardoning power, ordinance power, and emergency roles. This topic is important because it connects constitutional design with parliamentary government.

## Practice Questions
- How is the President elected?
- Why is the President called the nominal executive?
- What is the scope of the President's pardoning power?
- How is the President connected to Parliament?
- What exam trap appears in questions on ordinance power?
