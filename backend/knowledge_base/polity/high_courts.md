# High Courts

## Simple Explanation
High Courts are the highest courts at the state level. They supervise the administration of justice within their jurisdiction and have broad powers to issue writs and review decisions of subordinate courts.

## Key Points
- Every state or group of states has a High Court.
- High Courts have original, appellate, and supervisory jurisdiction.
- They can issue writs not only for Fundamental Rights but also for other legal rights.
- High Courts supervise subordinate courts under their jurisdiction.
- UPSC often tests the distinction between High Court writ power and Supreme Court writ power.

## Exam Relevance
This topic is important because it links state judiciary, writ jurisdiction, and judicial independence. It is commonly asked in comparison-based questions involving the Supreme Court, subordinate courts, and constitutional remedies.

## Practice Questions
- How is the writ jurisdiction of a High Court wider than that of the Supreme Court?
- Why are High Courts important for judicial administration in states?
- What is meant by supervisory jurisdiction of a High Court?
- Can one High Court serve more than one state?
- Why does UPSC compare Article 226 with Article 32?
