# Non-Cooperation Movement

## Simple Explanation
The Non-Cooperation Movement was launched by Mahatma Gandhi in 1920 to oppose colonial rule through peaceful non-cooperation. It aimed to withdraw popular support from British institutions and transform nationalism into a broad mass movement.

## Key Points
- The movement combined the Khilafat issue, Punjab grievances, and the wider demand for self-rule.
- Its programme included boycott of schools, courts, councils, titles, and foreign cloth.
- It brought students, lawyers, peasants, traders, and urban groups into a wider political struggle.
- Gandhi suspended the movement after the Chauri Chaura incident because of violence.
- Even though it ended early, it decisively broadened the social base of Indian nationalism.

## Exam Relevance
UPSC commonly tests the causes, methods, spread, suspension, and significance of the Non-Cooperation Movement. It is also important for understanding Gandhian strategy and the rise of mass nationalism.

## Practice Questions
- Why did the Non-Cooperation Movement become a mass movement?
- What was the political logic behind non-cooperation with colonial institutions?
- Why was the movement suspended after Chauri Chaura?
- How did the movement change the character of Indian nationalism?
- How does UPSC distinguish between the movement's achievements and its limitations?
