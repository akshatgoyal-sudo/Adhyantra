# Public Service Commissions

## Simple Explanation
Public Service Commissions are constitutional bodies that help ensure merit-based recruitment to civil services. The Union Public Service Commission and the State Public Service Commissions protect fairness and professional standards in public appointments.

## Key Points
- The UPSC serves the Union, while State Public Service Commissions serve the states.
- These commissions conduct examinations and advise on recruitment matters.
- Their constitutional status supports neutrality in public employment.
- They also advise on promotions, transfers, and disciplinary matters in certain cases.
- UPSC often asks about the independence and advisory role of Public Service Commissions.

## Exam Relevance
This topic matters because it connects constitutional governance with the civil services system. Questions often test composition, functions, reporting, and differences between Union and State Public Service Commissions.

## Practice Questions
- Why are Public Service Commissions important for a merit-based bureaucracy?
- How is the UPSC different from State Public Service Commissions?
- Why are these commissions described as advisory bodies?
- What kind of matters are referred to Public Service Commissions?
- Why does constitutional status matter for recruitment institutions?
