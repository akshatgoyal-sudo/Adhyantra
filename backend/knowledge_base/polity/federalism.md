# Federalism

## Simple Explanation
Federalism is the constitutional division of powers between the Union and the states. In India, this structure is federal in form but has a strong unitary bias for maintaining national unity and coordination.

## Key Points
- Legislative powers are divided through the Union, State, and Concurrent Lists.
- The Constitution creates a federal structure with a strong Centre.
- Emergency provisions and residuary powers reflect the unitary tilt.
- Institutions such as the Inter-State Council and Finance Commission support cooperative federalism.
- UPSC often asks whether India is truly federal or quasi-federal.

## Exam Relevance
Federalism is important for both static Polity and governance questions. It helps answer prelims on constitutional lists and mains on Centre-state relations, cooperative federalism, and institutional balance.

## Practice Questions
- Why is Indian federalism often called quasi-federal?
- How are legislative powers divided in the Constitution?
- What shows the strong-unitary features of Indian federalism?
- How does cooperative federalism operate in practice?
- Why does UPSC connect federalism with contemporary governance debates?
