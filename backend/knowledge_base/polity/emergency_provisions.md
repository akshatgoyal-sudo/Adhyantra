# Emergency Provisions

## Simple Explanation
Emergency provisions allow the constitutional system to respond to extraordinary threats such as war, breakdown of constitutional machinery, or financial instability. They temporarily alter the normal balance of federalism, rights, and executive power.

## Key Points
- The Constitution provides national, state, and financial emergency mechanisms.
- Different emergencies affect rights, federal relations, and executive authority in different ways.
- National Emergency can significantly expand Union power and affect certain Fundamental Rights.
- President's Rule concerns failure of constitutional machinery in a state.
- UPSC often tests articles, effects, safeguards, and the distinction between types of emergencies.

## Exam Relevance
This topic matters because it combines constitutional text with historical misuse and later safeguards. Questions often focus on articles, effects on rights, and the federal consequences of emergency powers.

## Practice Questions
- What is the difference between National Emergency and President's Rule?
- How can emergency provisions affect federalism?
- Which rights are most discussed in emergency-related questions?
- Why were safeguards strengthened after past misuse?
- What is the role of Parliament during emergencies?
