# Constitutional Amendments

## Simple Explanation
Constitutional amendments are formal changes made to the Constitution through Article 368 and related procedures. They allow adaptation over time while preserving constitutional continuity.

## Key Points
- Different provisions require different amendment procedures.
- Some amendments need only a special majority in Parliament.
- Some amendments also require ratification by at least half of the state legislatures.
- The basic structure doctrine limits Parliament's amending power.
- UPSC often asks about amendment procedure and the effect of landmark cases.

## Exam Relevance
This topic is crucial because it combines constitutional procedure with judicial doctrine. Questions commonly test which provisions need state ratification and how the basic structure doctrine restrains Parliament.

## Practice Questions
- What is the role of Article 368 in constitutional amendments?
- Why do some amendments require state ratification?
- What is the basic structure doctrine?
- Can Parliament amend any part of the Constitution without limit?
- Why is Kesavananda Bharati central to this topic?
