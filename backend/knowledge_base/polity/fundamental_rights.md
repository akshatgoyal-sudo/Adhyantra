# Fundamental Rights

## Simple Explanation
Fundamental Rights are basic constitutional guarantees that protect liberty, equality, and dignity. They limit arbitrary state action and make citizenship meaningful in everyday life.

## Key Points
- They are mainly contained in Part III of the Constitution.
- Some rights are available only to citizens, while others are available to all persons.
- Many rights are subject to reasonable restrictions for security, public order, morality, and similar grounds.
- Article 32 allows people to approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of these rights.
- UPSC often asks about the difference between rights, restrictions, and constitutional remedies.

## Exam Relevance
This topic is central to both prelims and mains because it connects articles, landmark ideas, writ remedies, and the broader philosophy of constitutionalism. Questions often test scope, exceptions, and comparison with Directive Principles.

## Practice Questions
- Why is Article 32 considered a powerful constitutional safeguard?
- Which Fundamental Rights are available only to citizens?
- How do reasonable restrictions change the meaning of freedom under Article 19?
- Why does UPSC compare Fundamental Rights with Directive Principles?
- What is the role of writs in protecting individual liberty?
